# Reverse 3D (Unrender)

Input: a finished video. Only the first 10 seconds are used, or 60 with the visitor's own key (node 11, Video Slice, set per clip; the browser trims to the same length).

Output: a rough grey Blender-viewport blocking playblast at 16 fps, made with open-source models only: Qwen Image Edit draws the look, Wan 2.1 VACE renders the video.

## Where it runs and who pays

Unrender submits to Comfy Cloud itself (`https://cloud.comfy.org`, the same Comfy API v2 as a deployment; `src/lib/unrender-cloud.ts`), not to the serverless deployment, which lacks the node packs and models.
- **The visitor's own Comfy API key:** the still and the video run on their Comfy Cloud account and spend their credits. No limit on how many; clips up to 60 seconds (the browser compresses the clip to fit the 4.5 MB upload). The browser sends the key with each job poll and download as `x-comfy-key`, because the job lives on that account. Their account needs a Comfy Cloud plan that runs workflows.
- **An email signed in with a code:** one free video, the first 10 seconds, on the app's `COMFY_API_KEY_TEAM`.

Past 10 seconds VACE renders in passes of 161 frames inside the same job (`splitPlayblastPasses`): each pass gets its slice of the depth frames, the same still, prompts and seed, and the passes are joined before the colour comes out. The last pass is padded to 4n + 1 frames and cut back. Tested on Comfy Cloud with two passes of 81 frames: the join shows only a small jump. The side-by-side compare (original left, playblast right) is built in the browser by time, so the 16 fps playblast lines up with the original at any frame rate. The old Kling 4.0 Flash graph is kept as `workflow_kling_api.json`.

## The look: one still, then the video

1. **The still (`step=still`).** The browser grabs the clip's first frame (one frame in, 1024 px on the long side) and posts it to `POST /api/reverse-3d/subject`. One job (`buildStillWorkflow` in `src/lib/subject-pipeline.ts`): `LoadImage` → `ImageScaleToTotalPixels` (1 MP) → Qwen Image Edit 2511 lightning (4 steps, euler, cfg 1, AuraFlow shift 3) → `ImageDesaturate+` → `SaveImage`. The prompt redraws the exact shot as a low-detail grey blocking pass: faceless mannequins, objects kept as simple grey stand-ins, buildings as boxes, and nothing keeps its colour. The desaturate node takes out any colour the model leaves. About 9 GPU seconds.
2. **The video.** The browser re-encodes the still as a JPEG and posts it with the clip as `reference`, plus the clip's length in seconds. The still goes to node 30 (`LoadImage`), is desaturated again (31) and scaled to the video size (32), and VACE takes it as `reference_image`. If the still failed, the run route deletes nodes 30 to 32 and VACE renders from the depth pass and the prompt alone (`dropLookReference` in `src/lib/reverse-3d-look.ts`).
3. **The wait.** While VACE renders, the page un-renders the first frame into the still tile by tile, like a Cycles render running backwards (`src/components/tile-unrender.tsx`). The job reports progress only per sampler step, so the tiles also follow the time the job has taken and flip the rest when the video is ready.

## The graph

LoadVideo → Video Slice (first 10 s) → VideoFrameSample (uniform, the frames that make 16 fps) → GetVideoComponents → ImageScale (node 17, centre crop) → DepthAnythingV2 (node 40) → WanVaceToVideo (node 49: depth as `control_video`, the grey still as `reference_image`) → KSampler (4 steps, lcm, cfg 1, lightx2v LoRA, shift 8) → TrimVideoLatent (drops the reference frame) → VAEDecode → ImageDesaturate+ (node 33) → CreateVideo 16 fps → SaveVideo.

The run route fits the graph to each clip (`applyPlayblastShape`): the frame keeps the clip's shape with its longer side at 832 and both sides on 16-pixel blocks (1920×1080 becomes 832×464, 960×720 becomes 832×624), and the frame count is the clip's length at 16 fps: up to 10 s as 4n + 1, from 17 to 161 (161 for 10 s), longer clips every frame (480 for 30 s, in three passes). The server reads the size from the file (MP4/MOV sample size with the rotation matrix, WebM PixelWidth/PixelHeight) and the length from the container, capped by the length the browser sends.

Models the graph uses (all on Comfy Cloud): `wan2.1_vace_14B_fp16`, `Wan21_T2V_14B_lightx2v_cfg_step_distill_lora_rank32`, `umt5_xxl_fp16`, `wan_2.1_vae`, `depth_anything_v2_vitl.pth`, `qwen_image_edit_2511_fp8_e4m3fn_scaled_lightning_comfyui`, `qwen_2.5_vl_7b_fp8_scaled`, `qwen_image_vae`, and the comfyui_essentials nodes (`ImageDesaturate+`).

Measured on Comfy Cloud (RTX Pro 6000): a 10 second clip at 832×464 took 112.5 GPU seconds for the video, about 32 credits at the measured 0.29 credits per GPU second. A 5 second clip is about 15 credits. The Kling graph cost about 400 credits for 10 seconds.

`src/lib/reverse-3d-switch.ts` holds `REVERSE_3D_RENDERS_VIDEO`. While it is `false`, `POST /api/run/reverse-3d` answers 503 `video_off` before reading the upload, so no GPU time is spent. Mock runs stay open for local testing.

## The old 3D blockout

The page no longer calls these jobs; the subject route still serves them. They are kept here for reference.

**Finding everyone (`step=pick`).** Up to 12 frames across the accepted clip, 512 px on the long side, go to `POST /api/reverse-3d/subject`. The graph is `GeminiNodeV3` (`Gemini 3.5 Flash`, thinking `LOW`, 4096 output tokens) with one image per frame, then `SaveText`. The reply chooses the frame and lists every person, animal and vehicle on it, also ones cut off by the edge or out of focus, plus up to 3 objects someone sits on, rides, holds or carries. At most 8, the most important first. Each has a name, a kind, a box `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]` in 0-1000 and its real height in meters. Tiny boxes and repeats of the same kind are dropped. A reply with no frame number is an error, not a guess.

**Marking the poses (`step=mark`).** The browser grabs the chosen frame once at full size and cuts everything from it: the plate behind the blocks (1280 px), the frame for the model (1024 px) and one close-up per subject. A close-up is the subject's box with a margin of 14% of its longer side, between 0.6 and 2.2 wide over tall, slid inside the frame and scaled evenly to 768 px on its long side, so the model marks joints on a subject that fills the picture instead of on a few pixels of the frame. The second `GeminiNodeV3` job (16384 output tokens) gets the frame as image 1 and the close-ups as images 2 to 9, each with the subject's box inside it. It marks each subject on its own close-up: a person's head, torso, hips, upper arms, forearms, thighs, shins, hands and feet; an animal's head, neck, body, legs and tail; a car's lower body, cabin and wheels; a bike's wheels, its frame as one bar from the seat to the handlebar, and its fork; a motorbike's wheels and body; an object's 1 to 4 pieces. A part is its long axis from joint to joint (`a`, `b`), its width over that length (`w`), its depth over its width (`d`) and its side (`z`: 1 nearer the camera, -1 farther). The browser maps the points from each close-up back onto the frame. A subject left unmarked, and an object whose marks stray outside its box (they went onto someone sitting on it), is one block over its box. If the second job fails, everyone is still built, one block each.

**The blocks.** Each part is a box along its axis, turned to follow the pose. People are never straightened; objects a few degrees off level or plumb are, and vehicles only within about 3 degrees, because a car seen at an angle really slants. Every subject first covers itself on one plane seen from the starting camera, then slides along the camera's rays to its own distance and is scaled with them, so from the starting camera nothing moves. What the camera sees of a block is its front face, so that face is the one sized to cover the frame: sized for its middle, a car's deep body looked a tenth too big. The distance is its real height over how tall it looks, against the middle subject, from 0.75 to 1.4 times as far, squeezed toward the middle because the height guesses are rough. A person cut off by the frame gets their whole height from the parts that show. A car keeps Gemini's line through its body, slant and ends included, but Gemini marks the body about twice as thick as it is, so the body is at most half as thick as the car is tall and the cabin stands level on it, up to the roof. Wheels are cylinders: as wide as they look when they face the camera (round from the side, an oval at an angle), or showing the tread when a wheel looks narrower than 0.35 of its height. A car seen from one side gets its two hidden wheels on the far side, as big and as high as the near ones, straight behind them from the camera. An object over 45% of the frame is the background and is left out. The frame stands on the plane the camera turns around and is drawn first, so blocks behind it still show. Up to about a hundred blocks land in about 6.5 seconds, then the camera sways to show the depth, less for a deep scene so nothing slides far off its picture.

**Where it looks wrong.** Gemini can put a joint a little off, swap left and right on someone seen from behind, leave out a limb hidden behind the body, or misjudge how wide a wheel looks. Depth comes from guessed real heights, so a child next to an adult stands too far back. The blocks line up with the frame only from the starting camera; turning shows the guessed depths. A container with no duration and no cluster timestamps is rejected; a file whose timestamps claim a short length is accepted even if the pictures run longer.

**Cost and time.** About 1 credit for the first job and 1.5 to 16 for the second (more close-ups cost more), so about 3 to 17 credits per upload, in 30 to 60 seconds. No paid job is part of the checks: mock mode answers both jobs with a sample reply without calling Comfy.
