# Reverse 3D

Input: a finished video, at least 3 seconds. Only the first 5 seconds are used (node 11, Video Slice, and the browser trims to the same 5 seconds). Kling 4.0 Edit allows 3-30 seconds and keeps the input length; the 5 second cap keeps one run near 200 credits.

Output: a rough grey Blender-viewport blocking playblast via Kling 4.0 Flash Edit. The side-by-side compare (original left, playblast right) is built in the browser so the graph does not OOM stitching frames. The first frame is dropped on both sides because Kling's frame 0 is often smeared.

## The look: one still, then the video

1. **The still (`step=still`).** The browser grabs the clip's first frame (one frame in, 1024 px on the long side) and posts it to `POST /api/reverse-3d/subject`. One job: `LoadImage` → `GeminiNanoBanana2V2` (`Nano Banana 2 Lite`, 1K, thinking `MINIMAL`) → `SaveImage`. The prompt redraws the exact shot as a low-detail grey blocking pass: faceless mannequins, objects kept as simple grey stand-ins and never turned into people, buildings as boxes. About 9 credits and 10 to 20 seconds.
2. **The video.** The browser re-encodes the still as a JPEG and posts it with the clip as `reference`. It goes to node 30 (`LoadImage`) and into Kling as `model.reference_images.image_1`, and the prompt tells Kling to copy the look of `@Image1` on every frame. If the still failed, the run route deletes node 30 and swaps in a prompt that describes the same look without `@Image1` (`src/lib/reverse-3d-look.ts`).
3. **The wait.** While Kling renders, the page un-renders the first frame into the still tile by tile, like a Cycles render running backwards (`src/components/tile-unrender.tsx`). Kling rarely reports progress, so the tiles also follow the time the job has taken and flip the rest when the video is ready.

Tested on a 5 second, 4:3 clip of four people and a bicycle: every frame kept the still's look and the bicycle stayed a bicycle. Kling 4.0 Flash is about 38 credits per second of video, so a 5 second clip costs about 190 credits plus 9 for the still.

## The graph

LoadVideo → Video Slice (first 5 seconds) → GetVideoComponents → ResizeAndPadImage → CreateVideo (constant 24 fps, original audio) → Kling4EditVideoNode (with node 30's still) → SaveVideo. Browser MediaRecorder WebMs often have no duration and confuse LoadVideo into reporting 1 fps; re-wrapping the decoded frames at 24 fps keeps the frame count and satisfies Kling's 24 fps minimum without stitching (which OOMs).

Kling 4.0 Edit requires both sides between 700 and 4553 pixels and a width/height ratio from 0.40 to 2.50. Node 17 runs on every clip. The server sets its canvas from the file: MP4/MOV sample size, swapped when the track matrix is 90° or 270° so it matches the frames LoadVideo rotates, and WebM PixelWidth/PixelHeight even when the Segment size is the 8-byte unknown length Chrome MediaRecorder writes. It scales uniformly until both sides are at least 700, keeps an already-legal frame as-is up to 1920×1080, and pads with black only when the ratio is outside 0.40–2.50 (smallest legal canvas, 700×1750 or 1750×700). Nothing is cropped or stretched. The long side stays at or under 1920 so a 10 second 24 fps batch does not grow toward Kling’s 4553px ceiling.

`src/lib/reverse-3d-switch.ts` holds `REVERSE_3D_RENDERS_VIDEO`. While it is `false`, `POST /api/run/reverse-3d` answers 503 `video_off` before reading the upload, so no Kling credits are spent. Mock runs stay open for local testing.

## The old 3D blockout

The page no longer calls these jobs; the subject route still serves them. They are kept here for reference.

**Finding everyone (`step=pick`).** Up to 12 frames across the accepted clip, 512 px on the long side, go to `POST /api/reverse-3d/subject`. The graph is `GeminiNodeV3` (`Gemini 3.5 Flash`, thinking `LOW`, 4096 output tokens) with one image per frame, then `SaveText`. The reply chooses the frame and lists every person, animal and vehicle on it, also ones cut off by the edge or out of focus, plus up to 3 objects someone sits on, rides, holds or carries. At most 8, the most important first. Each has a name, a kind, a box `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]` in 0-1000 and its real height in meters. Tiny boxes and repeats of the same kind are dropped. A reply with no frame number is an error, not a guess.

**Marking the poses (`step=mark`).** The browser grabs the chosen frame once at full size and cuts everything from it: the plate behind the blocks (1280 px), the frame for the model (1024 px) and one close-up per subject. A close-up is the subject's box with a margin of 14% of its longer side, between 0.6 and 2.2 wide over tall, slid inside the frame and scaled evenly to 768 px on its long side, so the model marks joints on a subject that fills the picture instead of on a few pixels of the frame. The second `GeminiNodeV3` job (16384 output tokens) gets the frame as image 1 and the close-ups as images 2 to 9, each with the subject's box inside it. It marks each subject on its own close-up: a person's head, torso, hips, upper arms, forearms, thighs, shins, hands and feet; an animal's head, neck, body, legs and tail; a car's lower body, cabin and wheels; a bike's wheels, its frame as one bar from the seat to the handlebar, and its fork; a motorbike's wheels and body; an object's 1 to 4 pieces. A part is its long axis from joint to joint (`a`, `b`), its width over that length (`w`), its depth over its width (`d`) and its side (`z`: 1 nearer the camera, -1 farther). The browser maps the points from each close-up back onto the frame. A subject left unmarked, and an object whose marks stray outside its box (they went onto someone sitting on it), is one block over its box. If the second job fails, everyone is still built, one block each.

**The blocks.** Each part is a box along its axis, turned to follow the pose. People are never straightened; objects a few degrees off level or plumb are, and vehicles only within about 3 degrees, because a car seen at an angle really slants. Every subject first covers itself on one plane seen from the starting camera, then slides along the camera's rays to its own distance and is scaled with them, so from the starting camera nothing moves. What the camera sees of a block is its front face, so that face is the one sized to cover the frame: sized for its middle, a car's deep body looked a tenth too big. The distance is its real height over how tall it looks, against the middle subject, from 0.75 to 1.4 times as far, squeezed toward the middle because the height guesses are rough. A person cut off by the frame gets their whole height from the parts that show. A car keeps Gemini's line through its body, slant and ends included, but Gemini marks the body about twice as thick as it is, so the body is at most half as thick as the car is tall and the cabin stands level on it, up to the roof. Wheels are cylinders: as wide as they look when they face the camera (round from the side, an oval at an angle), or showing the tread when a wheel looks narrower than 0.35 of its height. A car seen from one side gets its two hidden wheels on the far side, as big and as high as the near ones, straight behind them from the camera. An object over 45% of the frame is the background and is left out. The frame stands on the plane the camera turns around and is drawn first, so blocks behind it still show. Up to about a hundred blocks land in about 6.5 seconds, then the camera sways to show the depth, less for a deep scene so nothing slides far off its picture.

**Where it looks wrong.** Gemini can put a joint a little off, swap left and right on someone seen from behind, leave out a limb hidden behind the body, or misjudge how wide a wheel looks. Depth comes from guessed real heights, so a child next to an adult stands too far back. The blocks line up with the frame only from the starting camera; turning shows the guessed depths. A container with no duration and no cluster timestamps is rejected; a file whose timestamps claim a short length is accepted even if the pictures run longer.

**Cost and time.** About 1 credit for the first job and 1.5 to 16 for the second (more close-ups cost more), so about 3 to 17 credits per upload, in 30 to 60 seconds. No paid job is part of the checks: mock mode answers both jobs with a sample reply without calling Comfy.
